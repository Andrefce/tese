\chapter*{Abstract}

\selectlanguage{english}
\noindent
Short-axis cardiac magnetic resonance imaging provides detailed observations
of the left ventricle on spatially separated slices, leaving the geometry
between them unknown. Reconstructing both ventricular boundaries is necessary
to estimate myocardial wall thickness beyond the acquired planes, but
interpolation alone does not guarantee anatomical accuracy.

This dissertation proposes a machine learning approach for three-dimensional
left ventricular reconstruction from magnetic resonance contours. An implicit
neural representation describes the endocardial and epicardial surfaces at
end-diastole and end-systole. The model is trained on synthetic ventricular
shapes and subsequently fine-tuned using surfaces fitted to clinical
segmentations. The resulting geometry supports local thickness maps and
regional analysis using the 17-segment model of the American Heart Association
(AHA).

Comparisons with radial basis function reconstructions and a statistical
shape model show greater agreement in the ventricular cavity than in the
myocardial wall. Thickness estimates depend on both the reconstructed geometry
and the measurement method, so high correlation does not necessarily imply
agreement between values. Regional analysis also characterises changes in
thickness between the two cardiac phases.

Without an independent three-dimensional anatomical reference, the evaluation
cannot determine which approach reconstructs the true anatomy most accurately.
The findings highlight the importance of considering reconstruction and
measurement choices when interpreting myocardial wall thickness.

\vspace{1em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Medical machine learning; cardiac magnetic
resonance; three-dimensional reconstruction; myocardial wall thickness;
implicit neural representations; cardiac modelling.
