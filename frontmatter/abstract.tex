\chapter*{Abstract}

\selectlanguage{english}
\noindent
The analysis of left ventricular geometry and myocardial wall thickness
is relevant to the characterisation of cardiac structure and function.
However, short-axis cardiac magnetic resonance imaging provides
a two-dimensional representation through spatially separated slices,
making three-dimensional reconstruction necessary to recover the
geometry between available slices. Although interpolation methods
establish continuity between contours, they do not guarantee the
preservation of ventricular anatomy. Furthermore, clinical assessment
of myocardial wall thickness often relies on local measurements and
visual analysis. In this context, the 17-segment model of the American
Heart Association (AHA) provides a reference for regional assessment
of the left ventricle.

This dissertation proposes a machine learning approach for three-dimensional
left ventricular reconstruction from cardiac magnetic resonance
contours. An implicit neural representation is used to continuously
describe the endocardial and epicardial surfaces at end-diastole
and end-systole. The model is initially trained on synthetic
ventricular shapes and subsequently fine-tuned using clinical
segmentations. Myocardial wall thickness is estimated from the
reconstructed geometry, enabling local and regional analysis
according to the 17 AHA segments.

The proposed approach is evaluated against radial basis function
methods and a statistical shape model. The results show that the
reconstructed surfaces follow the observed contours, with greater
agreement between methods in the ventricular cavity than in the
myocardial wall. Thickness estimates are also found to depend on
both the reconstructed geometry and the calculation method, meaning
that high correlation does not necessarily imply agreement between
measurements. Segmental analysis further characterises regional
variations in wall thickness between end-diastole and end-systole.

This work explores the potential of implicit neural representations
for three-dimensional left ventricular reconstruction and myocardial
wall thickness quantification. However, in the absence of an
independent three-dimensional anatomical reference, it is not possible
to conclusively determine which method produces the reconstruction
closest to the true anatomy. Nevertheless, the results highlight
the influence of geometric modelling choices on thickness estimates
and the importance of considering the reconstruction method when
interpreting the resulting measurements.

\vspace{1em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Cardiac magnetic resonance imaging;
left ventricle; three-dimensional reconstruction; implicit neural
representations; signed distance functions; myocardial wall thickness.
