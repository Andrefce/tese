Honest verdict on the new version

This version is substantially better. You fixed many of the most dangerous scientific issues from the previous draft. The thesis is now much more defensible because it is clearer about what the model predicts, what the reference represents, and what the results do not prove.

However, it is still not submission-ready, mainly because Chapter 5 and the front matter are unfinished, and because the evaluation still lacks a reconstruction baseline or ablation.

What you fixed well
1. You significantly reduced the circularity problem

This is the biggest improvement.

The new version explicitly states that the encoder receives contours extracted from the original acquired clinical slices, not contours obtained by re-slicing the derived mesh. The derived mesh is used only as training supervision.

That is much stronger than the previous version.

Your wording is good:

“This preserves the acquisition geometry and prevents the derived mesh from leaking into the encoder input.”

This does not completely eliminate dependence between the input and surrogate target because both originate from the same segmentation, but it removes the worst form of leakage.

2. “Ground truth” has been replaced with “surrogate target”

This is exactly the right correction.

The new version consistently explains that the meshes are:

segmentation-derived;
affected by interpolation, smoothing and capping;
not independently acquired anatomy;
suitable for reconstruction-consistency evaluation, not anatomical validation.

The sentence added to the methodology is particularly strong:

“The contribution is therefore a method for geometrical completion and reconstruction consistency from sparse observations. It does not establish independently validated anatomical truth.”

Keep that.

3. The decoder offset is no longer incorrectly presented as wall thickness

You now distinguish between:

the decoder field offset δ\delta;
geometric wall thickness measured between reconstructed surfaces;
the calibrated offset used only for visualisation.

This is a major scientific improvement.

The strongest corrected sentence is:

“It is a learned difference between field outputs, not a Euclidean transmural distance measured between the extracted surfaces.”

Likewise, you now openly state that calibration fixes the mean “by construction” and is not independent evidence of anatomical accuracy. That resolves one of the most damaging issues in the previous version.

4. Training contradictions are mostly resolved

You now clearly report:

approximately 200 pre-training epochs;
776 fine-tuning epochs;
checkpoint selected at epoch 695;
cosine annealing with warm restarts;
T0=100T_0=100;
batch size 8 per GPU;
two GPUs;
two accumulation steps;
effective batch size 32;
augmentation on both synthetic and real training observations.

This is much more reproducible and fixes the 400-versus-776 epoch contradiction.

5. The M&Ms dataset distinction is clearer

You now explicitly say that M&Ms-2 is used and that the Campello et al. 2021 paper describes a separate 375-study dataset and is not being used as the citation for the M&Ms-2 release.

That is honest, although you still need the correct citation for M&Ms-2.

6. The model-versus-voxel comparison is useful

Table 4.2 now applies the same thickness methods to both:

model-reconstructed geometry;
segmentation-derived voxel geometry.

That is much better than comparing model measurements only with one generic mask anchor.

The interpretation is also appropriately cautious:

“The reconstruction broadly preserves between-patient variation while shifting the absolute thickness scale.”

That is a solid, defensible finding.

2. There is still no reconstruction baseline

Section 4.1.2 is called “Baselines,” but it does not contain a competing reconstruction method. It only defines:

the surrogate target;
the four wall-thickness estimators;
the mask-based thickness anchor.

That is not a reconstruction baseline.

You are still unable to answer:

Why use this neural implicit model instead of conventional interpolation?

At minimum, compare against one simple method:

isotropic interpolation of the segmentation stack followed by smoothing and marching cubes; or
contour lofting/spline interpolation.

Because your surrogate mesh is already generated by an interpolation and smoothing pipeline, you may treat that pipeline as a non-learning comparator—but you must actually compute its performance under a fair sparse-input protocol.

3. No ablation validates the architecture’s key contributions

The discussion says:

“The evaluation supports one central design choice of the model…”

But it does not.

You demonstrate that the final model produces positive field offsets. You do not demonstrate that the coupled parameterisation is better than independent fields.

To support the design choice, you need:

full model;
model with independent endocardial/epicardial heads.

Then compare:

field collapse rate;
surface intersection rate;
containment failures;
reconstruction metrics;
thickness consistency.

Similarly, there is no evidence that:

synthetic pre-training helps;
phase conditioning helps;
the local 16316^3 volume helps;
Fourier features help.
Minimum useful ablation

If you can run only one:

Full model versus identical architecture trained without synthetic pre-training.

That directly validates a major contribution and should be relatively straightforward.

If you can run two:

Without synthetic pre-training.
Independent surface heads instead of positive field coupling.
4. Some old incorrect language remains

Although most terminology was corrected, a few passages still revert to stronger claims.

Problematic sentence in Section 3.3

“The epicardium is defined as an outward offset from the endocardium, so the wall remains positive everywhere and the model learns a valid myocardial thickness field instead of two independent surfaces.”

This contradicts your later, more accurate explanation that δ\delta is not anatomical thickness.

Replace it with:

“The epicardial field is defined through a positive offset from the endocardial field, preventing collapse of the two predicted fields. Anatomical wall thickness is subsequently measured between the extracted surfaces.”

Problematic sentence in the Discussion

“The predicted wall offset is positive everywhere, so a thickness value exists at every myocardial point and never collapses to zero.”

Again, δ\delta is not a thickness value.

Replace with:

“The predicted field offset is positive at every evaluated point, preventing the two implicit fields from collapsing onto one another. Geometric wall thickness is subsequently estimated between the extracted surfaces.”

Problematic objective

The introduction still says the objective is:

“to ensure that the reconstructed surfaces are smooth and closed, with a myocardial wall of positive thickness.”

You cannot fully ensure this through the field parameterisation because mesh topology is affected by discretisation and repair.

Use:

“to encourage smooth, closed reconstructed surfaces and prevent collapse of the endocardial and epicardial implicit fields.”

5. Watertightness is still reported too favourably

You now say “100% after the repair stage” in the body, which is good. But Table 4.1’s caption says:

“Every reconstruction in the cohort is watertight on both surfaces.”

That should say:

“Every reconstruction is watertight after the evaluation repair stage.”

Better still, report:

raw watertight rate;
repaired watertight rate;
number of cases remeshed from occupancy;
number of holes filled;
mean volume change due to repair.

Without raw statistics, 100% watertightness tells us primarily that the repair procedure works.

Also avoid saying:

“The watertightness that motivated the signed-distance formulation is also achieved…”

Use:

“Watertight evaluation meshes are obtained after applying the repair procedure…”

If selected manually or for convenience, disclose it.

7. Your strongest correlations need fuller statistical reporting

You report correlations of r=0.99r=0.99 between model and voxel-based per-patient mean thickness values. That sounds extremely strong, but you do not provide:

confidence intervals;
scatter plots;
regression slopes;
intercepts;
Bland–Altman bias for model versus voxel geometry;
per-patient sample size confirmation.

A correlation of 0.99 can coexist with systematic scaling or common dependence on subject size.

You already acknowledge an absolute shift. Show it properly:

one scatter plot per method;
identity line y=xy=x;
regression line;
patient-level Bland–Altman plot;
bias and limits of agreement.

This would make the result much more convincing.

8. Table 4.3 may still have a clustering problem

It says the methods are compared:

“over the myocardial surface points of the 30 patients.”

If you pool all vertices together, the vertices are not independent. Thousands of vertices from a single heart share the same geometry.

You must state whether:

agreement was calculated separately per patient and then averaged;
all vertices were pooled;
the ICC used patient clusters;
vertices were subsampled equally per patient.

If pooled, rewrite the limitation clearly and preferably recalculate at the patient level.

At minimum:

“Because surface vertices within a patient are spatially correlated, the point-level agreement statistics describe numerical consistency over the reconstructed fields and must not be interpreted as estimates based on independent observations.”

9. Literature review still needs updating

The review says it covers publications through 2026, but the bibliography appears to end around 2021.

That now looks particularly problematic because there is at least closely related work on 3D cardiac reconstruction from sparse 2D cardiac MRI. Your novelty claim is narrow enough to survive, but the related-work section needs to discuss the closest cardiac reconstruction methods rather than relying mainly on Pixel2Mesh, Pose2Mesh and generic ShapeNet work.

You need a subsection such as:

Sparse cardiac MRI to 3D cardiac geometry

Then explain precisely how your work differs:

contour rather than image input;
paired endocardial and epicardial outputs;
implicit rather than fixed-topology output;
phase conditioning;
synthetic SSM pre-training;
positive field separation;
wall-thickness analysis.

Also either provide the correct official citation for M&Ms-2 or explicitly cite the dataset release page/data descriptor.

Important scientific wording correction

Your strongest overall thesis claim should be approximately:

This work presents a phase-conditioned implicit reconstruction pipeline that converts sparse endocardial and epicardial contours extracted from clinical SAX segmentations into continuous paired LV surfaces. The model is trained using synthetic SSM-derived geometry and segmentation-derived surrogate meshes and is evaluated for reconstruction consistency against held-out surrogate targets. The resulting surfaces support locally resolved computational wall-thickness analysis, but the study does not establish independent anatomical or clinical validity.

That is accurate and still sounds like a meaningful contribution.

Bottom line

This version is much more scientifically mature. The biggest conceptual problems from the prior draft—mesh-to-input leakage, treating surrogate geometry as ground truth, and treating δ\delta as direct anatomical thickness—have been addressed well.

The major remaining weakness is now clearer:

You have demonstrated that the final pipeline behaves consistently, but you have not demonstrated that its architectural choices improve upon simpler alternatives.

If Chapter 5 is completed, the remaining wording is corrected, and at least one baseline or ablation is added, this could become a strong master’s thesis.
