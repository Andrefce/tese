\chapter{Conclusions}
\label{ch:conclusions}

\noindent
This chapter summarises the work and answers the three research questions
of~\cref{sec:research-questions} on the evidence of~\cref{ch:results}.
It then states the contributions, limitations, and directions for future work.

\section{Summary of the Work}
\label{sec:conclusion-summary}
\noindent
Wall thickness is measured on the three-dimensional LV, but SAX cardiac MRI
records separate slices with an in-plane pixel size below 2~mm and several
millimetres between them~\parencite{bernard2018acdc,kramer2020cmrprotocols}.
The wall between slices must therefore be reconstructed. A method was developed
to recover both ventricular surfaces from these contours and measure wall
thickness beyond the acquired slices.

The proposed 3D reconstruction model represents the LV surfaces as continuous
signed-distance fields~\parencite{deepsdf2019}. It was first pre-trained on
synthetic shapes sampled from an SSM~\parencite{bai2015biventricularatlas}
to learn the general ventricular form, then fine-tuned on ED and ES surfaces
fitted to ACDC~\parencite{bernard2018acdc} and
M\&Ms-2~\parencite{martinisla2023mnms2} segmentations to capture differences
between patients and cardiac phases.

The proposedmodel combines a global PointNet pathway~\parencite{qi2017pointnet},
which summarises the overall shape, with a local feature
grid~\parencite{peng2020convonet}, which retains nearby contour information.
Cardiac phase is supplied as input so that one network reconstructs both ED
and ES. The epicardial field is defined by subtracting a strictly positive
learned offset from the predicted endocardial signed-distance field, preventing
crossing in the continuous representation. The fields are then converted into
meshes for measurement.

The reconstruction was compared with an RBF implicit
fit~\parencite{carr2001rbf} and an SSM fit to the same rings, examining contour
fidelity, agreement between methods, and sensitivity to slice removal. Without
an independent three-dimensional reference, these comparisons describe
consistency rather than establish which surface is closest to the true anatomy.

Myocardial wall thickness was measured on each geometry with four local
methods~\parencite{saito1994edt,jones2000laplace,yezzi2003thickness,shapira2008sdf}.
Comparisons across geometries and within the same geometry separated the
effects of reconstruction and measurement. Surface maps and AHA
17-segment summaries~\parencite{cerqueira2002segmentation} showed regional
thickness, changes from ED to ES, and differences between normal and
HCM~\parencite{ommen2020hcm} patients.

\section{Answers to the Research Questions}
\label{sec:conclusion-research-questions}
\noindent
\textbf{RQ1} compared the learned reconstruction with RBF and SSM fits to the
same contours. The proposed model follows its input to within about one in-plane pixel
(1.50~mm on the endocardium and 1.96~mm on the epicardium), lies closer to
the RBF fit than to the SSM fit, and, unlike the SSM, keeps a similar contour
distance on normal and HCM cases. The three methods agree on the cavity but
not on the wall. The learned reconstruction performs comparably to conventional
fitting and handles abnormal anatomy more evenly than the SSM, but is not more
robust to slice removal, which degrades all three by similar amounts.

\textbf{RQ2} asked whether the proposed phase-conditioned model can reconstruct
paired endocardial and epicardial surfaces at ED and ES while maintaining a
positive wall thickness. The answer is a qualified yes. One network
reconstructs both phases, the epicardial field cannot cross the endocardial
field by construction, and the reconstructed wall thickens in every AHA
segment, from 9.21~mm at ED to 13.87~mm at ES. Pointwise separation on the
extracted meshes and accuracy at ES were not verified.

\textbf{RQ3} asked how strongly wall thickness depends on the reconstruction
prior and the measurement method, and whether regional and pathology-related
differences are retained. Both factors matter. The prior mainly shifts the
absolute scale while largely preserving the ranking of patients, and a
straight-ray measurement departs strongly from methods that follow the curved
wall. Absolute thickness values are therefore comparable only within the same
reconstruction and measurement pipeline. The reconstructed geometry does retain
group differences: the HCM group is 3.67~mm thicker than the normal group in
patient-level mean ED thickness, whereas relative systolic thickening does not
separate the groups. With ten HCM patients, this is a descriptive result rather
than a diagnostic one.

\section{Contributions}
\label{sec:conclusion-contributions}
\noindent
This work makes three contributions. First, a shared data pipeline combines
synthetic SSM meshes with clinical ED and ES segmentations from two public
datasets. It stores contour rings, surface samples, signed-distance targets,
and wall-offset targets in one format for both pre-training and fine-tuning.
Second, the model processes endocardial and epicardial contours separately,
combines global shape and local contour information, and uses a supervised
positive offset to keep the epicardial field outside the endocardial field.
Third, an evaluation approach applicable to other networks assesses contour
fidelity, agreement between reconstructions, and sensitivity to slice removal.
It also compares thickness methods on the same geometry, with AHA summaries
and patient-group comparisons.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion-limitations}
\noindent
The limitations in~\cref{sec:threats} bound these answers. Without an independent
3D reference, geometric results measure contour fidelity and agreement between
methods, while thickness results measure reproducibility rather than accuracy.
The pathology cohort is small, and no ablation experiment isolates the
contribution of individual model components.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion-future-work}
\noindent
These limitations set the order of the next steps. The first need is an
anatomical reference. Higher-resolution volumetric acquisitions, or expert
three-dimensional surface and wall-thickness annotations on a larger external
cohort, would turn the agreement figures reported here into accuracy figures
and would show which of the three priors lies closest to the true wall. The
second is a wider and fully matched comparison, adding spline
lofting~\parencite{fuchs1977contours}, Poisson
reconstruction~\parencite{kazhdan2006poisson} and learned
alternatives~\parencite{occupancynetworks2019,muffoletto2025nihc} under the
same patients, physical spacing, repair procedure and metrics, with error maps
at the apex, base and most curved parts of the wall. Controlled tests should
then remove
synthetic pre-training, local conditioning, phase conditioning and the
positive-offset coupling one at a time to isolate their contributions.
Pointwise separation and intersection checks on extracted meshes would address
the remaining geometric checks in RQ2. Replacing the binary phase label with a
continuous position in the cardiac cycle would allow coherence in time to be
tested alongside continuity in space.

In sum, the proposed model turns separated SAX contours into closed, paired LV
surfaces that honour the observations, thicken consistently from diastole to
systole, and support local wall-thickness analysis. Disagreement between
equally plausible reconstructions nevertheless shows how much of the wall
remains inferred rather than observed, and how carefully its thickness must be
interpreted.
